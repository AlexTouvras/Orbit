## The question

After years in credit risk and analytics, I stepped into a technology delivery lead role. People asked whether I was "leaving data behind." I wasn't — the job changed how I use it.

## What stayed the same

Quantitative habits don't disappear. I still want **evidence before decisions**: operational metrics, dependency maps, incident patterns, and clear definitions of done. The models I built taught me to ask *what could break in production* — that lens applies to middleware and Azure services too.

## What changed

The unit of work is no longer a notebook or a dashboard. It's **coordination**:

- Turning vague initiatives into sequenced delivery with visible flow.
- Making trade-offs explicit when platform, security, and business timelines collide.
- Translating between engineers, operators, and stakeholders who use different vocabularies.

I'm less often the person writing the SQL. More often the person making sure the right SQL gets written, reviewed, and shipped on time.

## The bridge I didn't expect

Risk work trained me for **uncertainty under constraints**. Delivery work rewards the same muscle: you rarely have perfect information, but you still need a call, a rollback plan, and a way to measure whether it worked.

## What I'm documenting next

This site is part of that shift — a public record of what I build and learn on the engineering side (agents, automation, this platform) while leading delivery day to day. If you're making a similar move from analytics to operations leadership, the through-line is simpler than it looks: **make the system legible**.
