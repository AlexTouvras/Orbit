## The question

Can a Monday morning portfolio review fit on **one screen** without losing the signals that matter for credit risk?

## What we were doing before

Spreadsheets, emailed extracts, and a rotating cast of "temporary" tabs. Everyone had a slightly different cut of the same data. By the time leadership met, half the meeting was reconciling numbers instead of deciding what to do.

## What the dashboard optimizes for

I designed around **three decisions**, not thirty metrics:

1. **Is emerging risk accelerating?** — vintage trends and segment drift, not just headline rates.
2. **Where is the engine behaving differently than the model assumed?** — approval funnel and score distribution shifts.
3. **What changed since last week that we didn't expect?** — annotated exceptions, not raw tables.

Everything else links out. If a metric doesn't change a decision, it doesn't earn space above the fold.

## Technical choices that mattered

- **SQL views as the contract** — Power BI reads stable, documented shapes; logic doesn't live in DAX spaghetti.
- **Fabric / refresh discipline** — predictable morning refresh beats heroic manual runs.
- **One owner per metric** — every tile has a name attached. Ambiguity kills trust faster than bad charts.

## Mistakes I made

- Too many slicers early on. Executives don't want to build their own report at 8 a.m.
- Letting "just one more breakdown" creep in. Scope creep applies to dashboards too.
- Skipping the **definition doc**. If two teams define "delinquency" differently, your dashboard becomes a debate club.

## Takeaway

The best operational dashboard isn't the prettiest — it's the one people **trust on Monday**. Start with the decision, work backward to the query, and delete anything that doesn't earn its pixel.
