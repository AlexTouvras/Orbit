The art of the picture

A point of view, not a chart

Dwell → claim → sketch → lead → open

A data story is a point of view drawn so a stranger can feel it. The number already had a life in it: a person, a place, a decision. The work is to spend time until that life is nameable, then to say one sentence, then to draw a mark that belongs to that sentence. Giorgia Lupi calls this data humanism. Data is a language. It is taken, not given. It is imperfect. The picture should admit that.

Alberto Cairo’s warning belongs on the same card. “Story” is not a licence to omit the inconvenient window, start the axis where the drama starts, or glue a beginning, middle, and end onto a picture that argues by where the marks sit. Truthful, then functional, then beautiful, then insightful, then enlightening. Beauty is the invitation. It comes after honesty, or it is a trick.

Chart type is a lane. A bar is the honest lyric when the sentence is simply “this is larger.” A custom mark is earned when the standard chart cannot carry the life. Grain and the semantic model stay on the Analytics card. Cutover stays on Delivery. Essays stay prose.

What has to be written down

The life You can name who, or what decision, is inside the number. If you cannot, you have not dwelled.
The sentence Say it with the picture covered. One sentence. A second claim is a second picture.
The doubt The miss, the gap, the window you refused — visible in the drawing, not only under it.

Ladder + gates

  1. Dwell until a life is nameable
  2. One sentence, said aloud
  3. A sketch that is not a chart-library screenshot
  4. The eye’s first stop, and the turn
  5. The doubt left in the picture
  6. Analytics if the grain is wrong — Delivery if the release is the job
Kill switch If you cannot name the life, the sentence, and the doubt, it is a chart. It is not a story yet.

Anti-patterns

  • A chart gallery titled “story”
  • A hero’s journey glued onto a distribution
  • A custom metaphor that makes “larger” harder to see
  • Sixteen filters and no sentence
  • Beauty that moves the axis to where the drama starts
  • A footnote doing the work of the drawing
  • An essay turned into a scroll animation — essays stay prose
  • Calling the grain a story — that is the Analytics card
