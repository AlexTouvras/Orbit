## Editorial contract

Every page:

1. Starts from the decision or failure the reader has, then the subject. The opening of Data that drives decisions starts from the capability instead: the path a number walks when a person can act. A failure is not the first thing on that page.
2. Says why the practice works, what it assumes, and when another approach fits.
3. Gives a procedure, a decision, a check, or another artifact the reader can use.
4. Separates established guidance, the author's rule, and a hypothesis. A synthetic scene says so in its first line.
5. Adds something the other pages do not already say.

One thesis per page. Headings follow the subject. They are not a fixed list of eight. A page is as long as the argument: a principle can be short, a method needs the day and the limit, a deep dive needs the mechanism. Do not add length to look finished. On screen that argument continues across leaves of one size.

Voice: direct, operational, first person for the author's judgment. Define a term a stranger may not know. No corporate filler. A memorable sentence is rare, and it has to clarify the rule.

Evidence: cite the primary source, with its date or version, and only for a claim that source makes. A short link sits beside the claim and opens the section when the source has one. The page ends with one list of the works. Do not invent a metric, a client, or a result. A number you did not open does not go on the page. Say how you would tell the practice is working. That is a measure to track, not a result you do not have. Put the date you last checked the claims on the page.

A figure is allowed when it carries the procedure: a loop, a decision, a shape. A photograph is allowed when it draws that section and states no amount the prose does not. It belongs to that book. The words still work if the figure or the photograph does not. No motion required to understand the page. The standard is the contract above. The diagrams, the section count, and the length change with the subject.

A picture has one of two jobs. A design picture sits behind the words on the same leaf. The words stay readable, and the picture does not name a step. A narrative picture shows what that section says, in words a reader can use. Those are the pictures that matter. What good looks like is the test. The room is behind the words. The invoice path is drawn as Ask, Grain, Truth, and Use. Later pages of this book still use their current pictures until they are revised the same way.

A written chapter ends once on why the practice still holds when AI drafts, checks, or explains the work. The reason is the decision and the person who will act. A claim that fails that test is rewritten where it stands. AI stays in that close.

### Shape of Data that drives decisions

In force 18 Oct 2026. The field card is the framework. The book runs that card in six parts. Ask, Grain, Truth, and Use are the test for one number. They are not the outline of the practice.

1. What good looks like. Place the work, and place the organization on the card's ladder: ad hoc, defined, managed, optimized. Written at `/1`.
2. Choose the work. A case to build, or a written reason not to. No route. No source has been read for a prioritisation method.
3. Make the number fit to use. The brief, what one row means, a disagreement between two reports, the owner and the columns, and whether the person can explain the number and act. The written sections are Name the decision `/2`, Say what one row means `/3`, When two reports don't match `/4`, and Keep the definition `/5`.
4. Choose the stack. The plates and the lanes for a named workload, including what was refused. No route. No source has been read for a selection method beyond the card.
5. Keep trust in force. Security, governance, observability, evals, and human approve, for the numbers that share a system. No route. Lineage, freshness, security, and a method for completeness, validity, and timeliness have no source yet.
6. Run the product and judge the value. Continue the product, change it, or stop the investment. No route. No source has been read for judging the investment.

A row with no route is not published. Do not add Hand the number over. One illustration runs through the book: whether to give an invoice more time to pay. It states no amount. The book does not open on a failure.

Scale is whether the number still arrives, for the people who use it, inside the time the decision needs. It is named in Choose the stack, refused in Choose the work, and stopped in the value review. Maturity uses the card's four rungs. Descriptive, diagnostic, predictive, and prescriptive name the kind of question.

Inside Make the number fit to use, the four steps still name the sections.

**Ask — Frame the decision.** The business question, the outcome, the person who will act, and the evidence that would change what they do.

- Mapped: Name the decision, `/2`. The brief names the decision, the outcome, the actor, the constraint, a hypothesis if the number rises, a hypothesis if it falls, and the one activity. The hypotheses are proposals to check against the outcome.
- The page opens on the brief. The failure, a number that can be checked and still be the wrong answer, comes after the lines are named. The book's opening is What good looks like.
- Kimball's four-step design supports choosing the business activity before the grain. That is a modeling order, not a decision method. The GAO Results Act guide, April 1998, supports a measure that represents the goal it is supposed to assess. The guide is about an agency plan. The Outcome line is where this page writes that goal.
- The author's rule, not a cited method: name the decision, the outcome, the person, the constraint, and both hypotheses before the number goes out as the answer. The constraint, and the choice to treat the response as a hypothesis, are the author's practice.
- Gap: no source read establishes the constraint, or the rule that a response is a hypothesis. Do not attribute those lines to Kimball or to GAO. Do not stretch Kimball to cover the outcome as its own step. The outcome line uses the GAO link. It is not a second method.

**Grain — Define the measure.** The activity, what one row means, the time, what is included, what is left out, the dimensions that implement that row, and how the number is calculated.

- Mapped: Say what one row means, `/3`. The thing, the time, what is included, what is left out, and a sentence the user can say back. A list of columns is not that sentence.
- Not yet a section: the dimensions after that sentence, and the calculation. Kimball's four-step design already places dimensions and facts after the grain. That is the source when those sections are written. No route for them yet.
- Kimball, Declaring the Grain (1 Mar 2003), and the Kimball Group grain note support this layer. Kimball is a dimensional-modeling method. It supports how a measure is designed. It is not the authority for the decision, the reconciliation, or the action.

**Truth — Trustworthy evidence.** Quality, a comparison when two reports disagree, lineage, an owner, a test that can stop a bad number, and the control that keeps the definition.

- Mapped: When two reports don't match, `/4`. Compare, explain, or do not publish. Do not publish a number between the two reports. The GAO Green Book, May 2025, supports the comparison and a correction that happens in time. dbt's build and test severity support a failed test skipping what comes next, and a warning letting it run. The block, and the refusal to publish a midpoint, are the author's rule. The Green Book does not describe a dashboard.
- Mapped: Keep the definition, `/5`. The owner, the columns the number promises, and the stop when those columns break. dbt's model contracts and model versions support the column check and the breaking change. The owner is the author's rule. A green contract does not know what one row means.
- These two pages are sections of Make the number fit to use. They are not the opening of the book.
- Gap: lineage and freshness. The card names them. No handbook source has been read for them. OpenLineage is named on the card and is not yet a citation.

**Use — Turn evidence into action.** Explain the number, say what is uncertain, name the trade-off, recommend the move, and look at what happened after.

- Not written. No route. Hand the number over was a thinner name for this layer. Do not write that page under that title.
- The GAO guide supports a measure that helps someone decide and assign accountability. It does not teach how to explain uncertainty, or how to judge the outcome afterward. Choose a source for those claims before the chapter is written.

Name the decision, Say what one row means, When two reports don't match, and Keep the definition keep their prose. They are sections of Make the number fit to use. The chapter ends on Keep the definition, once, on why the brief, the row, the cause, and the owner still hold when AI drafts them. Use, as its own page, stays unwritten.
